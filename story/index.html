Chapter one

The problem.

I used AI for my job search, and the results were poor. Rejection letters piled up. Some applications asked me how I use AI in my own work.

Two questions followed. How do I search better? And how do I show, not tell, that I use AI well?

Chapter two

What I saw.

Then I started seeing repositories with tens of thousands of stars. A full design studio made of agents. A content workflow in a box. Impressive engineering.

I wanted to add what I know as a UX designer to something like that. Not another example project. A tool that is useful from the first day, calm, clear and under your control.

And easy for a creative with no AI background. No code. No puzzle of setup files. Nine steps and about 15 minutes.

Chapter three

What I made.

Not a bigger pile of agents. A careful one, made for people who are not AI experts, with three promises.

Gates before scores.

Remote, country, language, work rights and legitimacy are checked on the employer's own posting, with a quoted line. "Remote" on an aggregator card often means one country.

Facts only.

Every CV is built from one file about you. A check flags numbers, dates and names that file does not contain. Nothing is invented.

You press the button.

It never submits, sends a message, creates an account or enters a password. Drafts only. The last click is always yours.

A dashboard that tells the truth.

Your real odds, in plain words, with a range. Funnel, fit radar, and a calm line after a rejection.

Small tools for the boring parts.

Log an update one question at a time. Follow-up drafts in English or Spanish. A five minute weekly review.

An assistant that proposes.

It reads your email export and suggests tracker changes, each with a quote. You accept or skip.

Four skills, no code.

Screen, craft, apply and review, packaged for Claude. Craft tunes your CV and portfolio for each role. Install once, set up in about 30 minutes.

Chapter four

Built with AI.
In the open.

hireframe was designed by me and built with Claude. I set the direction, made the design decisions and reviewed what came back. Claude wrote code and drafts. I commit every change myself.

Every rule the AI follows is written in the repository, so you can read what it may and may not do.

Never submitsNever inventsNever sendsNever creates accounts

I also failed my own usability test. Every session I carried CSV files between the browser and a folder by hand. Nobody should have to do that. The dashboard now connects to your folder once, reads your files on its own, saves changes straight back, keeps a copy before it writes, and asks you before it overwrites anything Claude changed.

That is my answer to the question about AI at work. Not a promise. A working example.
